# Strawberry Care Guide

## Watering
Strawberries have shallow roots and need consistent moisture between 55% and 85%. Water early morning so leaves dry before evening. Never wet the leaves, flowers or fruit — water at the base. Inconsistent watering causes small, sour berries. During fruiting, moisture swings reduce fruit size sharply.

## Disease and fungal risk
Botrytis (grey mould) is the number one strawberry killer. Risk rises steeply when humidity stays above 75% for more than 6 hours, especially between 15-25°C. Prevention: space plants 30cm apart, remove old leaves and any mouldy berries immediately, improve airflow with a fan or pruning, never water in the evening. Powdery mildew appears in warm dry spells after humid periods — white powder on leaf undersides.

## Fertilizer and sweetness
Potassium drives sugar content and berry size — switch to a high-K feed (0-0-50 or seaweed liquid) two weeks before fruiting. Too much nitrogen gives lush leaves but watery, tasteless fruit. Calcium (gypsum or crushed eggshell) firms the fruit. Feed every 2 weeks during fruiting, monthly otherwise.

## Temperature and season
Ideal growing range is 18-24°C; above 28°C the plant stresses, flowers drop and fruit stays small. In India, plant runners October-November for a December-March harvest — cool dry months are peak season. Shade cloth (30-50%) helps in unexpected heat.

## Profit tips
Pick berries every 2 days at full colour — overripe fruit on the plant invites mould that spreads to good fruit. Morning-picked berries last longer in market. A single botrytis outbreak can take 30-50% of a harvest; prevention costs almost nothing.
