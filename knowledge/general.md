# General Plant Care Knowledge

## Watering principles
Water early morning (6-9 AM) — less evaporation loss, and leaves dry before night. Evening watering leaves foliage wet in darkness, the perfect fungal breeding ground. Deep infrequent watering beats shallow daily sprinkles: roots follow water downward and become drought-resistant. If rain is forecast within a few hours, skip watering — free irrigation.

## Reading moisture numbers
Below the crop's dry threshold: water soon, plant is entering stress. Above the wet threshold: stop watering, roots need oxygen — overwatering kills more plants than drought. Rapid moisture drops on hot afternoons are normal; a slow steady decline overnight is the true drying rate.

## Humidity and fungal risk
Most fungal diseases (grey mould, mildew, blight) need 6+ hours of humidity above 75% to establish. Airflow is the cheapest medicine: spacing, pruning, a small fan. If a humid spell is forecast, act the day before — remove dense foliage, avoid any leaf wetting.

## Heat stress
Above the crop's heat limit, plants close their pores and stop growing; water demand spikes then crashes. Water extra in early morning before a hot day, never at noon (leaf scorch through water droplets). Temporary shade (cloth, moved pots) during heatwaves protects flowers, which are always the first casualty.

## Saving money
Every skipped unnecessary watering saves water, electricity and labour. Rain-fed days are free — plan around the forecast. Prevention (airflow, timing, hygiene) costs minutes; disease outbreaks cost harvests. Track patterns month by month: knowing your farm's rhythm is the most profitable knowledge there is.
